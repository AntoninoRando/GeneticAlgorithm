Known quirks & open questions

Things that surprise newcomers, small inconsistencies, and the research questions still open. None of these is a crash bug. Several would change results if "fixed", so treat each fix as an algorithm change: literature, maths, seeded experiments.

Behaviour that affects results

The fitness rewards scheduling few, fast jobs

Response time is averaged over scheduled jobs only, and the min-max normalised cost terms outweigh any realistic difference in completion ratio. Being the costliest individual costs 0.40 fitness, while a 0.1 lead in \(C\) earns only 0.06. Long runs therefore lower \(R\) and \(C\). Derivation: theory §5. Evidence: results.

Opt-in fix: --fixed-bounds divides the costs by per-world bounds and charges every unscheduled job the worst response (theory §5). It is off by default and its effect on the test worlds has not been measured yet, so this quirk still describes the default run and every number in the thesis.

Energy is the binding constraint

Satellite budgets are 14–26 units (_rescale(…, 70, 130) * 0.2 in data_factory.py), the energy rate is 1 per minute, and job durations are 6–38 minutes. Each satellite can run one or two jobs; the 300–600-minute sunset almost never matters. That caps \(C\) at about 0.2–0.27 for every method and compresses the differences between rules. If you change the energy model, expect every number in the thesis to move.

Job-only rules pile onto the first satellite

The decoder breaks score ties by index. A rule that reads no satellite terminal (93 of the 280 final rules of the main study are the single terminal job.arrivalTime, and the FCFS and shortest-job-first baselines are job-only too) therefore fills satellite 0, then satellite 1, and so on, while later satellites are idle. Which satellite is "first" is just the order of the input file. Opt-in fix: --earliest-free-tie-break sends ties to the satellite that is free earliest (decoder, theory). It is off by default and not yet measured, and under tight batteries it can schedule fewer jobs, so this quirk still describes every number in the thesis.

Best fitness can go down even with elitism

Elites survive unchanged, but their fitness is recomputed against each new population's min and max, so the best fitness value can fall from one generation to the next. main.py's "10 generations without a new best" convergence rule is therefore a heuristic. With --fixed-bounds the fitness no longer depends on the population, the best fitness cannot fall while the worlds stay the same, and the rule becomes exact (a unit test checks it).

Ramped half-and-half couples method and depth

In TreeBuilder::rampedHalfAndHalf the depth is minDepth + i mod m (with \(m\) depths in the ramp) and the method is chosen by the parity of \(i\). When \(m\) is even (cap 7 gives depths 2–7, \(m = 6\)), the two are locked together: grow only ever gets depths 2, 4, 6 and full only 3, 5, 7. Koza's method uses both at every depth (koza1994gp §4). It does not happen with cap 4 (\(m = 3\)), the setting of the thesis experiments, but it does with the CLI default of 7. A fix (for example choosing the method from \(\lfloor i/m \rfloor\)) would change seeded runs at cap 7.

At the depth cap, crossover and mutation often do nothing

A child that exceeds the cap is replaced by a copy of its parent, and a mutated tree that exceeds it is restored whole, losing every other change of that mutation. With the thesis cap of 4 this is common: about one crossover child in three for two full trees, and 62% of subtree replacements at depth 3 (derivation). The GP textbook names this as a known flaw of rejection-based depth limits (poli2008fieldguide §11.3.2). It is one source of the parent copies that --clone-retries was added to fight. Opt-in fix: --depth-aware-crossover and --depth-aware-mutation never build an oversized tree (how). Both are off by default and not yet measured on the test worlds.

Hoist is stronger than it looks

More than half of all hoists yield a one-node tree (theory §6). Keep the hoist rate at or below 0.05, or restrict hoist to internal nodes.

"FCFS-like" rules are actually LIFO

The thesis (Evolved Rules section) calls job.arrivalTime FCFS-like. With higher-is-better scoring it is latest-arrival-first. The FCFS baseline is \(-a_j\). This is a wording fix in thesis/chapters/experiments.tex.

Four terminals never vary

sat.cpuBusyUntil, sat.networkBusyUntil, sat.energyReserved and sat.loadRatio are 0 for every satellite in our data: 4 of 18 terminals (7 job + 11 satellite), about 22% of uniform draws.

Open research questions

  1. Fitness design. Fixed-bound normalisation now exists as an option (--fixed-bounds) but has not been evaluated: run the fixed configurations of run_ablation.py and decide whether it becomes the default. Lexicographic (\(C\) first) and Pareto (NSGA-II) fitness are still untried. See theory §5.
  2. Deadlines. Jobs have no deadline, so there is no counterpart to the papers' deadline terminals and constraints (magliarisi2025orbit Eq. 7). This is the main gap with the literature.
  3. Dynamic decoding. The decoder is batch (all jobs visible at once). The papers make one decision per event. A dynamic version would match the real access point better.
  4. Multi-tree GP. Separate job-selection and satellite-selection trees (zhang2025mtgper, mtgp_multicloud).
  5. More data. All results come from one 300-minute trace. More traces, and loads where the sunset actually binds, would test generalisation properly.
  6. Justify or test the project additions marked "project" in the design map.
